# Bardstale prototype

Bardstale is a Bard's Tale III-inspired RPG with tile-based movement through connected voxel dungeons, caverns, woodland, and a village, an 8×8 character-grid interface, and turn-based encounters. It runs directly in the browser as JavaScript modules.

## Runtime structure

- `index.html` loads `main.js`, which creates the dungeon, scene, HUD, minimap, and gameplay systems, then handles input and animation.
- `src/dungeon.js` owns environment-specific map generation, house placement, directions, and wall access. `src/environments.js` defines the four areas; `src/worldTravel.js` creates and caches their connected maps and reciprocal exits.
- `src/render/scene.js` owns the Three.js scene lifecycle and environment lighting, then switches between first-person exploration and an orthographic combat camera.
- `src/render/voxelWorld.js` batches terrain and buildings from the masonry, landscape, and building model catalogs; `voxelEnvironment.js` batches furniture and nature props. `voxelTravelExits.js` draws passable exit markers and destination labels.
- `src/render/combatMapControls.js` handles combat map dragging, rotation, zoom, and view reset without interfering with click selection.
- `src/render/combatEffects.js` animates pixel projectiles, weapon strikes, spell impacts, healing, and protective particles in the shared scene.
- `src/render/combatLighting.js` follows combatants with warm local lights and rounded contact shadows.
- `src/render/voxelParts.js` defines immutable voxel part assets and character recipes with named anatomy and wearable slots. Parts have unrestricted integer coordinates and dimensions.
- `src/render/voxelHumanoidBody.js` sculpts one coherent human body and partitions it anatomically into torso, arms, and legs; clothing and armor use those same joints. `voxelCharacterParts.js`, `voxelEquipmentParts.js`, and `voxelCreatureParts.js` author the head and back variants, equipment, and distinct creature volumes.
- `src/render/combatVoxelCatalog.js` resolves every class and enemy, equipped items, and appearance choices into an assembled voxel model with bounds calculated from its occupied cells.
- `src/render/itemEffects.js` renders persistent equipment flames, crystal orbits, mist, and glow, with effects attached to the equipped part and shared by inventory and combat.
- `src/render/voxelMaterials.js` gives all voxel assets full RGB material response, continuous mineral and grain detail, and neutral reflections.
- `src/render/voxelFigure.js` merges exposed voxel faces into lit 3D meshes; `combatVoxelActors.js` owns actor placement, facing, motion, defeat poses, picking, and disposal.
- `src/render/voxelRig.js` assigns rigid shoulder and hip hinges, binds wearable and held equipment, and samples walking and action poses. The renderer meshes each hinge separately and keeps item particles attached to it.
- `voxel-study.html` is the shared voxel workshop, with the complete combat roster, part/color selectors, separated parts, and JSON export.
- `src/render/inventoryPanel.js` presents equipment, carried items, transfers, consumables, and a live preview using the combat voxel model. `inventory.css` styles its responsive layout.
- `src/render/hud.js` renders the party, combat log, actions, and character portraits, and forwards viewport clicks to tactical selection.
- `src/render/tiles.js` draws the bitmap alphabet, woven red frame tiles, and gold corner tiles. Interface text uses these pixels rather than browser fonts.
- `src/render/minimap.js` renders the explored map and party position.
- `src/items.js` is the shared item catalog: equipment slots, item statistics, stack limits, and voxel part selections.
- `src/systems/inventory.js` owns atomic bag and equipment mutations and character stat calculation.
- `src/systems/core.js` owns class, spell, song, ability, and monster definitions, party state, inventory services, encounters, and combat.
- `src/combatFootprints.js` defines occupied cells and centers, with a shared 2×2 footprint for dragons and ogres.
- `src/systems/battlefield.js` owns combat positions, movement paths, occupancy, and wall-aware targeting on the exploration dungeon.
- `config/uiSettings.js` defines the 80×50 cell grid, colors, and viewport slot.
- `config/combatSettings.js` defines the three combat cells per side of each dungeon tile.

`initBt3Systems({ hud, combatView, dungeon })` receives the rendering services and the current dungeon directly. It returns `isCombatActive`, `onStepComplete(depth)`, `handleCombatKey(key)`, and `selectCombatCell(x, y)` for the input loop. Its combat and inventory state is private to the systems instance. Inventory callers use `getInventorySnapshot(characterId)`, `previewInventoryEquipment`, `equipInventoryItem`, `unequipInventoryItem`, `transferInventoryItem`, `useInventoryItem`, and `subscribeInventory`; subscriptions return an unsubscribe function. Combat snapshots drive the scene and HUD; the renderer does not own gameplay rules. The combat view provides `show`, `hide`, and asynchronous `playEffect` services. Actions wait for their visual effect before applying their result and advancing the turn.

## Current gameplay

The starting party contains Brynn the Warrior, Ser Ana the Paladin, Shade the Rogue, Lyre the Bard, and Quint the Chronomancer. Each character starts with equipment and an individual inventory.

Completed movement steps can trigger encounters from all five monster tiers, weighted 5:4:3:2:1 toward easier foes. Tier-two roaming groups contain at most two foes; higher-tier creatures appear alone, and retreat is available from every roaming encounter. An explicit URL depth restricts encounters to that tier and retains its original group sizes and retreat rules. Each dungeon tile contains a 3×3 combat grid, with room for up to nine single-cell combatants. Encounters place the party and monsters on connected combat cells around the party's actual position. Each living hero can move up to four combat cells and take one action per round: attack, cast a spell, sing, use an ability, retreat, or end their turn. Heroes can be selected in any order; switching preserves each hero's remaining movement and action. Once every eligible hero finishes, monsters move up to three combat cells toward the party and act. Movement follows the legal path one cell at a time, with each step visible before the next. Input stays locked during movement and action effects, while camera controls remain available. Equipment and temporary effects contribute to combat statistics. Victory awards gold and may add a healing potion.

Melee requires a neighboring combat cell across an open passage. Longbows and ranged magic require line of sight through the dungeon walls and a range of six combat cells. Original dungeon walls block all three combat edges along their tile boundary; open passages and doors allow crossing. Living units cannot overlap or walk through each other. Dragons and ogres occupy 2×2 combat cells; placement reserves all four cells, movement checks the entire body against walls and occupants, and attacks can reach any exposed edge. Clicking any occupied cell selects the same creature. Defeat releases the full footprint, and revival requires all of it to be clear. Combat movement leaves the exploration map and position unchanged. Combat ends by regrouping the party at the encounter position and restoring the first-person camera and ceiling.

The prototype keeps game state in memory. Save/load, shops, and out-of-combat spell use are not implemented.

## Inventory and equipment

Each character has 24 carried inventory slots and 13 equipment slots: head, neck, shoulders, chest, hands, waist, legs, feet, two rings, back, main hand, and off hand. Equipped items are removed from the bag; swaps return the displaced items to it. Potions stack up to nine per slot, restore missing health or spell points, and consume one dose only when usable. Players can transfer carried stacks between party members. Full bags reject operations without losing or duplicating items.

Two-handed weapons return offhand gear to the bag; an offhand item cannot be equipped while wielding one. Short swords and daggers fit either hand, and a second blade contributes half its damage. All occupied equipment slots contribute statistics, including both rings. Armor class is better when lower. A bard's equipped back-slot lute enables songs; carrying it in the bag does not. The starting party carries spare armor, jewelry, weapons, and consumables for experimenting with different builds.

Press I or click Gear to open the inspected character's inventory. Select an item to inspect its properties, choose its destination slot, equip or remove it, use it, or give it to another hero. Party tabs switch characters, and rotation controls turn the live voxel preview. Escape or Close returns to the game. Inventory can be inspected during combat, but equipment, consumables, and transfers are locked until exploration resumes.

## Character interface

The interface uses a fixed 640×400 bitmap with 8×8 cells. The screen grows by whole integer multiples, with black space around it. Windows smaller than the native bitmap can scroll rather than compressing the glyphs or distorting the artwork.

Exploration fills the single left-hand frame in a square 256×256 slot backed by a 256×256 WebGL buffer. Its roster occupies the upper-right pane, with the selected character's portrait and statistics underneath. Combat switches to two full-height columns: a 360×384 battlefield on the left, with active-unit statistics, narration, and commands on the right. Its orthographic camera reuses the voxel masonry, hides the ceiling and upper wall courses, and renders physical voxel figures among the dungeon walls. Its WebGL buffer is 720×768 inside the unchanged 360×384 interface slot, keeping small shaded faces readable. Each original dungeon tile contains nine combat positions. The selected unit has a yellow frame, reachable movement cells are green, adjacent attack targets are red, and spell targets are cyan. Weapon strikes, flying bolts and arrows, spell bursts, healing, and wards use pixel sprites in the same scene. Portraits and particle effects use nearest-neighbor sampling; character geometry uses vertex colors and scene lighting. All frames, text, and click regions use cell coordinates. Control descriptions are available to assistive technology without taking space in the visual layout.

Outside combat, the lower pane shows narration or the current command menu. Combat keeps narration visible above its commands in the right column. Menus show up to eight numbered choices per page, with a separate Back command. Spell codes and SP costs appear before their names so the cost remains visible in the narrow column. Paging never removes a spell or target from the available choices. The map uses the same character grid and palette; large dungeons show a window around the party.

## Connected environments

The four areas form a returnable route: stone dungeon, crystal caverns, ancient woodland, market village, and back to the dungeon. Named compass markers identify exits; the minimap shows their destinations and coordinates. Stand on an exit and press Enter or its travel button. Travel keeps the same party, equipment, health, gold, and gameplay systems; returning restores the area’s discoveries and objects. Each area is generated once per journey. Travel is unavailable during combat, movement, or inventory inspection, and exit tiles do not trigger random encounters. The outgoing scene releases its GPU resources and resize listener before the next scene is built. Reloading the page starts a fresh journey.

Caverns have broad irregular chambers, layered rock, mineral veins, stalactites, crystals, boulders, and mushrooms. Woodland has open clearings, grass and earth, broad oak crowns, pines, ferns, and low thicket boundaries. The village has an open market square and enterable houses with timber, blue plaster, brick, or stone facades, windows, shutters, wooden interiors, pitched roofs, and chimneys. Houses vary from one to three storeys. Outdoor scenes use sky, daylight, and sun shadows; underground scenes use torchlight and closer fog.

All environments share the same reciprocal wall and open-door rules and three combat cells per map-tile side. Roofs, upper wall courses, and tall tree crowns cut away during combat. Tree trunks remain within one combat cell while their crowns can extend above adjacent cells; the generator preserves clear center paths and two-cell crossings for large creatures.

## Dungeon surfaces

Walls, floors, ceilings, and door arches are solid voxel masonry at the same voxel density as characters and furniture: 75 voxels per dungeon tile. Staggered stone courses have recessed mortar, chipped corners, and irregular relief; floor slabs sit above recessed joints while retaining a level walking plane. Ceiling ribs surround recessed stone panels. Open stone arches preserve traversable doorways. Combat hides the ceiling and upper wall courses, revealing the walls' solid cross-sections without stretching the stones. Furniture remains within its combat cell and shifts clear of adjoining stonework. Shared greedy surface meshes and spatial instance batches keep repeated masonry efficient.

## Character appearance

Exploration, combat, the workshop, and inventory previews use the same full RGB voxel materials and neutral reflection environment. Stone has mineral variation and damp lower courses, wood has directional grain, metal reflects light, ceramic has a glazed surface, and skin and fabric retain subtle color variation. Authored stone hues blend gradually across regions; furniture combines natural woods, patinated metals, varied foliage, and colored glazes. ACES tone mapping, cool shadow fill, and warm local lights keep material colors distinct in both game views. Combat retains softer ambient and directional fill so local torches and spell flashes stand out. The three torches nearest the active character cast real voxel silhouettes onto floors, walls, and other figures; the directional key covers the wider battlefield. The combat camera frames actual model bounds, including tall weapons and plumes. Effect impacts use torso height so they strike both small and tall creatures correctly. Local lights and small, faint contact shadows follow interpolated actor movement, and lights extinguish on defeat. The point-light shadow-map budget stays fixed as encounters grow, and unused maps are disposed when the active character changes. Figures move between cell centers, face movement and action targets in world space, and hold that direction when the camera rotates. Attacks, casting, and hits animate their pose; defeat tips and darkens the model, and revival restores it. Leaving combat disposes actor geometry and materials, effect resources, and combat lighting, then restores exploration.

Protective statuses display translucent domes and floor sigils; other magical statuses display colored rune circles. These visuals follow the affected character and disappear when the actual status expires or the character falls. Combat snapshots publish each unit's `auras` with its status ID, kind, color, and style. Cast effects carry source and target IDs and cells, with a rune style for spells. Projectiles leave trails, impacts briefly illuminate nearby stone, and confirmed HP loss produces blood particles and floor splatter. Death adds larger fragments. Blood remains on the battlefield until combat ends, when effects, auras, and decals are all cleared.

`assembleCombatVoxelModel({ kind, equipment, appearance })` resolves all 26 visual kinds: the 13 hero classes and 14 enemy species share one monk model. It returns a recipe, immutable part assets, assembled voxels, appearance, world scale, and combat footprint. For equipped characters, the shared item catalog supplies every visible wearable and held item; empty slots restore the natural body and plain underclothes. Unsupported kinds and item IDs are rejected. `createVoxelFigure(model)` renders the same data in battle or in a workshop layout with separated body parts.

Characters have head, torso, arms, legs, mainhand, offhand, back, shoulders, hands, feet, waist, neck, ring1, and ring2 anatomy slots. Chest items select torso and sleeve parts; gloves, shoulder armor, and boots remain independent overlays. Each equipped ring and necklace adds its own visible geometry. Magical items also carry visual effect descriptors: Ember Sword burns along its blade, Crystal Staff orbits its crystal with luminous particles, Venom Dagger releases green mist, Rime Mace releases blue mist, Void Shield releases violet mist, and Star Amulet glows. Effects follow item geometry through either hand and character poses, disappear with unequipped items, and extinguish on defeated actors. Particle envelopes are included in camera framing but never in collision or floor placement. Inventory animation stops when closed or hidden; reduced-motion preferences retain a static glow. Each figure uses at most one additional unshadowed light. The arms part contains both complete arms; legs can represent pants or a skirt. Every slot is optional and each asset may be any size. Torso swaps never replace arm geometry. Back parts include a cape, backpack, and lute. The bard carries the equipped lute on their back. Helmet plumes rise above the head, and staves, spears, and halberds extend above human height. Cloth, skin, bone, metal, wood, and magical surfaces store RGB colors directly. Serpents use coiled bodies, rat swarms contain multiple quadrupeds, and elementals have volcanic bodies and fire. A custom model can occupy the torso slot alone, as the creature models do. The dragon is a 48×39×47 voxel quadruped with wings, horns, claws, and a tail; ogres have bespoke anatomy roughly twice normal human height, sculpted with the same voxel size as humans. Dragons appear rarely while roaming, or in fixed depth-five encounters, and can breathe fire. Parts use signed integer voxel coordinates and optional offsets; later slots in the composition order (torso, legs, arms, head, back, shoulders, hands, feet, waist, neck, ring1, ring2, offhand, mainhand) own overlapping cells. Bounds come from the resulting geometry, while combat uses a constant voxel density so a larger item does not shrink the character.

Humanoid arms and legs rotate as rigid pieces at shoulder and hip joints. Travel drives alternating strides and arm counter-swings; melee actions wind up, swing at contact, and recover. Bows and spells have their own arm poses. Gloves, rings, shoulder armor, boots, weapons, shields, and item effects move with their owning limb. Pelvis, torso, head, and back items stay together; robes and kilts are cloth shells around the moving legs. The custom ogre uses authored joint tags within its single torso asset, including its club. Other custom creatures retain their whole-body poses. Living figures ground their body and feet independently of held weapons, and camera framing reserves a stable motion envelope. Reduced motion keeps limbs neutral, as does defeat before the whole-body fall.

Open `voxel-study.html` on the same server to inspect all heroes and enemies, mix humanoid parts, show part bounds, separate the parts, compare with a human reference, inspect the rear view, and export the actual recipe, scale, footprint, and referenced assets. Walk and Swing weapon preview the rigid joints; separate-parts mode stops these poses. Workshop selections are independent of gameplay equipment.

## Running and verification

Serve the VibeGames root over HTTP, for example with `python3 -m http.server 4173 --directory ..` from this folder, then open `http://localhost:4173/bardstale/`. Shared utility modules are loaded from the site and Three.js from its pinned CDN URL. Local art assets resolve relative to their owning modules. No external font service is needed.

Arrow keys move and turn while exploring; Enter travels when standing on a named exit. M opens the explored map, and M or Escape closes it. Click a roster row to inspect a character outside combat; I opens their inventory and Escape closes it. In combat, click a hero to select them and show their remaining movement range. Click a green cell to walk there or a red-framed enemy to attack. Choose a spell and click a cyan-framed target to cast it; friendly spell targets take priority over changing hero selection. Arrow keys also move the selected hero. Drag the map to pan, right-drag or Shift-drag to rotate, and use the wheel to zoom. Q/E rotate, plus/minus zoom, and Home restores the angle and fits the combatants. A controlled camera stays in place through gameplay updates. The remaining commands use the displayed 1–8 keys or canvas clicks; 0 selects Back. Page Up and Page Down turn command pages, and D turns character-detail pages. The URL accepts starting `environment` (`dungeon`, `cave`, `forest`, or `village`), `seed`, `w`/`width`, `h`/`height`, and optional `depth` (1–5); dungeon dimensions are limited to 8 through 128 cells. Without a depth override, all 14 monster species can appear during normal exploration. Use `?depth=3` to restrict encounters to knights, mages, and ogres, or `?depth=5` for liches, warlocks, elementals, and dragons.

Run `npm test` for dungeon generation, combat movement and actions, voxel assembly, equipment, and complete roster coverage, and `npm run check` for module syntax. No package installation is needed. After interactive changes, exercise movement, map toggling, encounters, and command selection in the browser, then check the console and capture screenshots.
